# 02 — Writing Samples: Prompts

> **Entity type:** `WritingSample` (corpus, role: prompt)
> **Source:** Raw user messages — Travis writing TO an AI agent.
> **Use:** Primary stylometric substrate. These are unedited Travis voice in his most natural register (informal-directive). Highest signal for diction, sentence rhythm, and lexical preferences.

The samples below preserve original capitalization, punctuation, and run-on structure. Annotations (in italics under each sample) flag stylistic signatures.

## Sample 1 — Initial ask (zed-workspace)

> i want to totally replace all my vscode based development tools with zed, and the most important part of that is workspace support that zed and all the other acp-driven ai tools will understand. that is the only feature that zed seems to be completely without

*Signatures:*
- Lowercase opening ("i want") even on a serious request
- Compound sentence joined with "and"
- Casual deictic closing ("that is the only feature...")
- Concrete and total: "totally replace all"
- Direct identification of the gap, not the wish ("the most important part of that is...")

## Voice Register (Two Modes)

Travis writes in two clearly distinguishable modes:

**Mode A — Casual Directive** (informal, fast, lowercase-friendly):
Used for: starting requests, redirects, quick corrections, mid-stream adjustments.
Example: *"i want to totally replace all my vscode based development tools with zed..."*

**Mode B — Architect Specification** (formal, structured, properly cased):
Used for: stakeholder-facing artifacts, brand documents, brief drafts, anything that will be read by Randy / Neil / Brittney / etc.
Example: *"Citizens National will have a named team — not a rotation of junior resources."*

The shift between modes is **context-dependent, not effort-dependent.** Travis is not "trying harder" in Mode B. Both are equally authentic. The substrate extractor must capture both.

## Correspondence Register (Derived)

> _Note: These patterns are derived from existing substrate cross-signals (personality profile, writing modes above, collaboration style in 10). No email samples exist in the substrate. Re-extract with actual email samples when available to improve Gate 3 register coverage._

### How the two writing modes adapt for a named human recipient

**Mode A (Casual Directive) → Correspondence informal:**
The same front-loading and direct-request structure carries over, but the frame shifts from "directing a tool" to "establishing a shared context with a person." Travis drops tool-naming and path-giving. The sentence rhythm stays fast. The opener goes straight to the point without relational clearing. There is no "hope you're doing well" before the business.

**Mode B (Architect Specification) → Correspondence formal:**
When writing to a named enterprise contact or formal external party, the mode-B density is preserved: full sentences, proper casing, structured paragraphs. The "etc." closer is absent. The "Talk about X..." imperative is absent — email is declarative, not directive. The close lands on a specific next step, not on an open invitation.

### What transfers from writing prompts to email

| Prompt feature | Transfers to email? | How it adapts |
|---|---|---|
| Front-loading context | Yes | Still leads with the situational anchor |
| Explicit sequencing ("I first want to...") | Yes (formal only) | Becomes paragraph order, not explicit statement |
| "etc." as casual closer | Selectively | Internal email only — removed in formal/external |
| Run-on chain with "and" | Internal only | Compressed to single-clause in formal contexts |
| ALL-CAPS for emphasis | Drops | Removed in email; emphasis shifts to word choice |
| Backticked paths / tool names | Drops | Tool and file references are not cited in external email |
| Parenthetical examples chained | Selectively | Retained in informal; pruned to one example in formal |
| Pivot to direct AI query | Drops | Email has no AI-query pivot |
| Hard-stop short sentence | Yes | Retained in all tiers — especially strong in internal email |
| Compound "and" linking | Yes (informal) | Reduced in formal |

### Salutation and sign-off by recipient tier

| Recipient tier | Salutation | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Internal team (Randy, Nicole) | None — starts with the point | None — ends on the action |
| External champion (Neil Henry tier) | First name only: "Neil," | `— Travis` or just `Travis` |
| Formal enterprise / first contact | "Hi [Name]," or none | `Travis James` + title if first exchange |
| Cold / unknown contact | "Hi [Name]," | `Travis James` + company + contact |

### Pattern templates

**Internal team email:**
```
[Point]. [Context if needed]. [What I need from you by when].
```
*Example structure:* "The CNB deck is due Thursday. Randy, I need the pricing tables and the competitive comparison updated before EOD tomorrow. Nicole, the cover page copy is still using the old Prometheus tagline."

**External champion email:**
```
[One-line relational anchor or update]. [Point + context]. [Next step I'm taking or what I need].
```
*Sign-off:* `— Travis` or `Travis`

**Formal enterprise / stakeholder email:**
```
[Named team referenced early]. [Business context in 2–3 sentences]. [Structured point]. [Explicit next step].
```
*Sign-off:* `Travis James` + title if first contact

## Ultra-Short Register (Derived)

> _Note: These patterns are derived from existing substrate cross-signals. No sub-280-character samples exist in the substrate. Re-extract with actual Twitter/X posts or social captions when available to improve Gate 3 register coverage._

### What survives 280-character compression

Compression is a filter, not a transformation. What it preserves is the hardest signal in the existing samples — the elements that carry meaning per character at the highest density.

| Element | Survives compression? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-stop short sentence | Yes — becomes the **core unit** | Already tightly packaged; often *is* the tweet |
| Reframe move ("Not A. B.") | Yes | Two-part structure fits in a single sentence |
| Specific numbers | Yes | Concrete beats general under constraint |
| Architecture anthropomorphization | Yes, abbreviated | "The agent doesn't know where it is." fits. |
| One Red intensifier | One per post | "genuine", "real", "perfect" — one, not stacked |
| Pointed comparison (one clause) | Yes, truncated | Implies the full comparison; doesn't state it |
| Named methodology / tool | Selectively | Works if the audience knows the term |

### What gets sacrificed

| Element | Sacrificed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Trade-off pairs | Almost always | No room for "strong claim, then quiet limit" |
| Parenthetical chains | Yes | "(e.g., like Chime, Varo, etc.)" costs 35 characters |
| "etc." as closer | Mostly | At 280 chars, "etc." reads as incomplete; drop it |
| Layered reasoning chain | Yes | Causals and "because" structures collapse |
| Numbered lists | Only if 2 items with punctuation | Lists don't scan well in social format |
| Multiple paragraphs | N/A | Single post = single thought |

### Characteristic ultra-short openers

Derived from the hard-stop pivot and "stop" command patterns in document 07:

- **Hard assertion opener:** A declarative sentence that makes a specific, countable claim. *("Context infrastructure is the missing layer.")* No preamble.
- **Reframe opener:** "Not [wrong thing]. [right thing]." *("Not a prompt. A context specification.")* Two sentences total.
- **Architectural failure opener:** Stating a failure mode flatly, letting the absurdity land. *("Agents don't know where they are. That's the entire problem.")* The pause between sentences carries the dry understatement.
- **Forward-projection opener:** A timing claim. *("The community banking core migration window is open now. It won't stay open.")* Closes on urgency without stating the word "urgency."

### What doesn't appear in ultra-short posts

- No "I'm excited to share..." openers
- No hashtag stacks (#fintech #AI #agentickprogramming)
- No rhetorical questions where the answer is "yes" ("Isn't it time we...?")
- No calls to action that ask for validation ("thoughts?" / "what do you think?")
- The closer, if any, is a stake — a consequence or a fact, not an invitation

### How documented written patterns surface in speech

Speech is not prose read aloud. It's a distinct delivery mode where some written patterns strengthen, some collapse, and some are replaced by analogs.

**Patterns that strengthen in speech:**
- **Architecture anthropomorphization** — "The agent doesn't know where it is. It's not stupid — it's blind." Spoken, with the pause between sentences, this lands harder than written. Travis can lean into it verbally with slight dramatization without overselling.
- **Hard-stop pivots** — The written period becomes a vocal pause. "He's not obstructionist. *[pause]* He's overwhelmed." The pause carries the dry understatement that the period implies in writing.
- **Specific numbers** — Numbers in speech create an anchoring moment. Travis's written tendency to cite specific figures ("3,549 MB") translates directly to spoken credibility signals.

**Patterns that convert to speech analogs:**
- **ALL-CAPS for emphasis** → **vocal stress** on the same word. ("MY platform" → "my **platform**")
- **Em-dash for interruption** → **actual pause or mid-sentence self-correction**
- **Parenthetical examples chained** → **listed aloud** ("...like Chime, Varo, that whole category")
- **"etc." verbal close** → still appears spoken, at the end of a list, same function — signals "I could name more but won't"

**Patterns that disappear in speech:**
- Backticked paths and tool names — not spoken
- Numbered list structure — replaced by sequence language ("first... then... after that...")
- Mode B's full sentence density — spoken mode naturally compresses; some formal precision yields to delivery

### Spoken-register behavioral markers

- **Starts sentences mid-thought:** "So the question is — and this is where most implementations go wrong — can the context survive a handoff?" Travis doesn't preface; he enters the thought in motion.
- **Self-corrects explicitly:** "The agent needs to know its location. Or rather, it needs to know what context it has access to — which is the same problem one level up." The "or rather..." is a spoken substrate marker. It signals real-time thinking, not scripted delivery.
- **Uses "etc." verbally:** "...all the context that would normally live in the IDE — project history, methodology, running notes, etc." Functions identically to the written version.
- **Architecture-anthropomorphization as a spoken framing device:** Treating systems as actors ("Cedar can't route on good intentions") is a natural spoken rhetorical device for Travis — it's vivid without requiring a slide.
- **Forward-projection close:** Spoken segments close on a stake or a next move. Not "does that make sense?" — that's a validation-seeking close. Travis closes on the implication or the opportunity.

### What informs the spoken register from 10-collaboration-style

- **Burst cadence:** Sessions are high-intensity; Travis thinks in connected blocks, not slow deliberate single points
- **Strategic fight style:** In Q&A or debate contexts, Travis assembles the case analytically. He doesn't get reactive — he goes more precise.
- **Direct register as default:** Even in conversational settings, Travis doesn't pad. No relational throat-clearing before the point.
- **"What specifically?" before answering vague questions:** In a spoken Q&A, if the question is vague, Travis surfaces the vagueness rather than answering the implied intent.
