## Pattern 1 — DARVO, run in real time

DARVO is the move where the accountable party flips the moral polarity of the conversation: the person who failed becomes the person who was wronged.

Watch the sequence:

- **You state the fact:** "you canceled twice without offering an alternative … that's disrespect."
- **She denies:** "I'm not sure what to say Travis … I enjoyed our time. I felt a spark." (No engagement with the actual claim.)
- **She attacks:** "You're projecting past relationships on me … You're not making me feel safe right now with your accusatory insensitive attitude."
- **She reverses victim/offender:** "I'm glad this is coming out now. Showing your true colors."

That's the textbook four-beat. The "safety" line is the load-bearing move — it converts your demand for accountability into evidence of *your* aggression, which means engaging with the substance of what you said is now framed as a violation of her emotional safety. It is a structural foreclosure of the accountability conversation. It is not a coincidence that the line shows up the moment the facts get specific.

A "spark" you felt is not an answer to "you stood me up twice." Those operate on two different layers. She kept answering on the feelings layer because the facts layer had no answer she could win.
