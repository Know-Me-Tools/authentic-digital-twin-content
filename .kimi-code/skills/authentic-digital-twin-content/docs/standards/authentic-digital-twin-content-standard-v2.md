# Authentic Digital Twin Content Standard v2

**Status:** v2.0 — published 2026-05-18. Supersedes v1 for new content. v1 articles remain valid at their declared version.
**Owner:** the author publishing under the standard. Each piece of content declares its version.

---

## What changed from v1

v1 defined a single annotation scheme — the italic eyebrow line plus a footer manifest — designed for long-form published articles where full annotation overhead is appropriate. v1 is complete and remains valid for that surface.

v2 extends the standard to the full range of communication surfaces an author produces. The core insight is that a full eyebrow-per-block manifest is correct for a 3,000-word article and wrong for a 280-character post or a real-time voice call. v2 introduces three annotation tiers matched to surface type, plus a surface taxonomy that maps every communication channel to the appropriate tier.

The three authorship categories are unchanged. The annotation *format* adapts to the surface; the *claim* the annotation makes does not.

---

## The three authorship categories (unchanged from v1)

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Human-authored** | Original content by the named author. No AI involvement, or AI involvement limited to spell-check and grammar that did not alter voice. The author certifies that the ideas, framing, and word choices are theirs. |
| **AI-drafted, human-edited** | Drafted by a named AI model via a named tool. The human author then edited substantively for voice, framing, accuracy, and structure. The published voice is the human's; the provenance of the original draft is reported. |
| **AI verbatim** | Generated by a named AI model via a named tool and reproduced without editorial intervention. |

## The three annotation tiers

### Tier 1 — Full manifest (long-form published artifacts)

**Applies to:** articles, blog posts, op-eds, newsletters, proposals, technical reports, whitepapers, internal memos intended for named authorship.

Same format as v1:
- Per-block italic eyebrow line immediately before each block
- Footer content provenance manifest table
- "How to read this article" section near the top

The full manifest is appropriate when the reader may scrutinize individual sections, when the piece will be republished or cited, or when the surface supports Markdown rendering.

### Tier 2 — Compact inline tag (short-form posts and correspondence)

**Applies to:** email (professional), LinkedIn posts, Twitter/X posts, Substack notes, Slack messages sent as authored content (as distinct from real-time chat), social media captions, internal announcements.

Format: a single compact disclosure tag at the **end** of the piece (email) or **beginning** (social posts), in parentheses.

Tag forms:

```
(AI-assisted, edited by <Author Name>)
(AI draft, unedited — <Provider> <Model>)
(<Author Name>, written without AI assistance)
```

The compact tag is appropriate when:
- The surface does not render Markdown reliably
- Per-block annotation would dominate the content (a 5-line email with 5 eyebrows)
- The communication is correspondence rather than publication

Tier 2 does not require a block-level manifest. A single disclosure covers the entire piece. The disclosure must be accurate: if the email contains both human-authored and AI-verbatim sections, the disclosure reports the dominant category or explicitly names both.

### Tier 3 — Channel-level disclosure (synchronous and ephemeral)

**Applies to:** voice calls, video presentations, real-time chat threads, meeting notes, voice memos, dictated content.

Format: a disclosure statement at the channel, session, or document level — not at the content block level.

Examples:
- In a meeting: "I prepared these talking points with AI assistance" stated once at the top
- On a voice transcript: a header note `[Preparation notes: AI-assisted draft, speaker edited]`
- In a chat thread: a pinned message or thread opener `These responses are AI-drafted; I review before sending`

Tier 3 is appropriate when:
- Real-time interaction makes per-block annotation impossible
- The content is ephemeral (not expected to persist as a citable artifact)
- The channel context already implies a lower expectation of solo authorship

Tier 3 disclosure is still mandatory when AI involvement occurred. The tier reduces the *granularity* of the disclosure, not the *fact* of it.

## Substrate register coverage (expanded in v2)

v1 required samples spanning at least two registers from the list: casual-directive, stakeholder-facing, technical, brand-facing, internal, external.

v2 adds three first-class registers required for full surface coverage:

| Register | Definition | When it activates |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoken / conversational** | How the author talks — pace, hedges, filler patterns, self-correction moves, informal vocabulary. Distinct from how they write. | Voice calls, podcast appearances, recorded talks, video presentations |
| **Ultra-short / micro-content** | How the author communicates in 280 characters or fewer. Compression moves, punch rhythms, what gets sacrificed, what stays. | Twitter/X, Substack Notes, social captions |
| **Correspondence** | How the author writes email and professional messaging. Salutation style, sign-off, level of formality per recipient tier, what they say vs. what they imply. | Professional email, Slack authored messages, LinkedIn DMs |

A substrate built only from long-form writing samples will produce voice-flat output when the author uses the skill to draft email, social posts, or prepare for a talk. The reliability gates are updated in v2 accordingly.

---

## Updated reliability gates (v2)

**Gate 1 — Sample volume (unchanged):** Combined word count of substrate documents 02 and 03 must be at least 2,000 words.

**Gate 2 — Register coverage (updated):** Samples must span at least two registers from the full register list (which now includes spoken, ultra-short, and correspondence). For Tier 2 and Tier 3 surfaces, the skill SHOULD warn if the substrate does not include the relevant new register, and MAY refuse to generate if the surface-specific register is entirely absent.

**Gate 3 — Surface-register match (new in v2):** Before generating for a specific surface, the skill checks whether the substrate covers that surface's register. Missing coverage triggers a warning:

```
Warning: No spoken/conversational register samples in substrate.
Output for voice surfaces will be based on written-register patterns.
Accuracy may be reduced. Add spoken samples to 02-writing-samples-prompts.md to improve.
```

The skill does not refuse generation on a Gate 3 failure — it proceeds with a warning, because the author may be intentionally using a cross-register approximation.

## Tiered annotation: what the format choices are and are not

The tiered format is not a looser standard for informal surfaces. The claim the author makes — "this is my voice" vs. "this is AI, I didn't edit it" — remains the same at all tiers. The format adapts to what the surface can carry without destroying the communication.

A Tier 2 disclosure on a LinkedIn post carries the same weight as a Tier 1 eyebrow in a published article. Both tell the reader which category applies. Neither allows the author to understate AI involvement.

---

## What "editing" means in short-form contexts

In a long-form article, editing is substantive when it changes voice, framing, or structure. For short-form surfaces:

**Email:** An AI-drafted email that was sent after one round of minor adjustments (changing the opening line and sign-off) is **AI verbatim** for the body, not `AI ← edited`. If the author rewrote the core argument or added a section, it is `AI ← edited`. The threshold is the same as in v1; the block is just the whole email rather than a section.

**Social post:** A 280-character post where the author accepted the AI draft and changed two words is **AI verbatim**. If the author rewrote the phrasing to match their rhythm, it is `AI ← edited`.

**Voice prep notes:** Notes the author prepared with AI and then used freely (not read verbatim) during a call are effectively `Author` — the author synthesized the material during delivery. Notes read verbatim from an AI draft with no editing are `AI verbatim`.

## Versioning

Articles and pieces produced under v2 declare `v2` in the "How to read" section (Tier 1) or in the disclosure tag (Tier 2). Tier 3 disclosures do not require a version number.

v1 articles do not need to be retroactively updated. v2 expands scope; it does not invalidate v1 content.

---

## Compliance check (v2)

A piece is v2-compliant if:

**For Tier 1 (long-form):** Same as v1 compliance check — all six points in v1 apply, plus the version declaration reads `v2`.

**For Tier 2 (short-form):**
1. A compact disclosure tag is present (at the end for email; at the top for posts)
2. The tag accurately reflects the dominant authorship category
3. If the piece is mixed (some sections human-authored, some AI verbatim), the disclosure names both categories

**For Tier 3 (synchronous/ephemeral):**
1. A channel-level, session-level, or document-level disclosure was made before or at the start of the content
2. The disclosure accurately states whether AI was involved and at what level
3. The disclosure is visible to the intended audience (not buried in a footer no one reads)

For all tiers: the authorship categories are used correctly, and AI involvement is not understated.
